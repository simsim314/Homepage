# Editorial guide

## Voice learned from the author interview

Use first person for the author's stated motives and recollections. Lead with the
problem, then explain what had to be built and why. Prefer direct, ordinary words:
“I wanted a simple tool” is a better starting point than a promotional claim.

Keep technical explanations concrete. A useful recurring structure is:

1. I wanted to answer a specific question.
2. Existing tools or constraints got in the way.
3. I built the infrastructure or mechanism needed to investigate it.
4. Here is what actually happened.
5. Here is what remains uncertain.

Preserve the author's distinctions and uncertainty. Do not rewrite an opinion as
community consensus. Clean spelling and punctuation without inventing emotion,
biography, motives, results, or conversations.

## Evidence and status

- Cite public source documentation; link specific papers only when verified.
- A cryptographic commitment is not verification of a mathematical theorem.
- A witness computation is not a complete deployed ZK system.
- A model's confidence score is not a published accuracy benchmark.
- A simulated gear does not establish a working physical prototype.
- Separate repository-reported results from independent reproduction.
- Article dates refer to publication of this site's text, not repository creation.
- Update dates change only when the article is substantively updated.
- Do not include or modify the separate Spirtuality repository.
