Agreed assembly convention, 2026-10-13

Complete the entire declared previous layer before beginning the next layer.
Bridging over an omitted tile is not allowed. Every input must connect to an
existing compatible output. A missing output is neither zero nor one.
An input marked "don't care" accepts either Boolean value, but still requires
a supplying connector. Designed separator spaces and socket clearance are not
missing tiles. No moving gates or other presence-detection hardware is proposed.

Layer sequence for the 26-type construction:
  starting black/white state; blue; orange horizontal; orange vertical;
  purple; new black/white state.

For the finite glider drawing, the input is a 5x5 patch with zero padding.
The blue layer consists of its 16 interior junction pieces. The first orange
layer has 36 strips over the central 3x3 target cells. The second orange layer
has 27 strips over those same target cells. Their required footprints are
declared explicitly; the exposed outer blue border is padding, not an omitted
orange tile. A larger simulation needs additional boundary treatment.

This convention enforces complete connections in the assembly model. It does
not assert that a person cannot physically rest a rigid piece over an empty
space. The precise 3D connection from the final rule layer to the lower,
preserved original-centre C column remains a separate geometry question.

Boundary extension, 2026-10-13:
The bounded-solids model extends the demonstration to all 5x5 target cells.
Four half-blue edge types and two quarter-blue corner types supply constant
outside zeros. These types may be used only on the declared outer perimeter,
rotated inward. Their only input sockets are the two/one on-board values.
All on-board padding cells evolve normally. Both Life-state layers have the
same 5x5 footprint. Infinite-plane agreement requires enough initial padding
for the chosen number of generations; the outside-zero boundary is fixed.
The stepped B/W solids now implement the original-centre connection.
