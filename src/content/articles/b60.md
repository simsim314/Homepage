B60 is a Herschel conduit I discovered in 2015 using [CatForce](/articles/catforce/). Its catalysts are just three blocks: **12 stationary live cells** supporting the reaction.

## Sixty generations

A Herschel input interacts with the blocks. After 60 generations, the output is another Herschel rotated by 180 degrees, displaced by $(-6,-10)$ relative to the input.

The catalyst blocks recover. Their role is to redirect the reaction without being permanently consumed.

## Closing the loop

Two copies of B60 can connect to one another. That closed loop underlies both the [Simkin glider gun](/articles/simkin-glider-gun/) and [Simkin's p60](/articles/simkins-p60/).

The gun and oscillator handle the emitted gliders differently. In the oscillator, additional eaters remove gliders that would interfere with the next Herschel.

B60 connects the search infrastructure to a concrete result: a compact catalytic reaction that becomes a reusable circuit element. LifeWiki documents its orientation, timing, recovery constraints, and pattern.
